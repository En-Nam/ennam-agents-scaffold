# Campaign brief playbook

A campaign brief exists to align everyone on one goal before money and time are spent. The discipline is subtraction: one goal, one audience, the fewest channels that reach them. Use this skill any time you draft a brief or review one before it runs.

## Structure

1. **Goal** — one measurable objective. Not "raise awareness" but "grow qualified signups: 400 → 600 (source: analytics)".
2. **Audience** — the specific segment this campaign targets, and the problem it speaks to.
3. **Channels** — the fewest channels that reach the audience. Each channel earns its place or is cut.
4. **KPIs** — the metric(s) that tell you it worked, each with a baseline and a target from a named source.
5. **Budget** — from the user, or `[BUDGET PLACEHOLDER]`. Never invent a spend figure.
6. **Timeline** — start, key milestones, end.

## One measurable goal

- A campaign with two goals has none. If you can't name the single number that must move, you're not ready to brief.
- The goal must be measurable at the end — an objective yes/no or a number, not a feeling.

## Sourced figures only

- Every baseline and target cites a named source (dashboard, analytics query, prior campaign). Never recall a number from memory; if it isn't in hand, write `[SOURCE TBD]` and surface it (Rule 13).
- Budget figures come from the user — use `[BUDGET PLACEHOLDER]` until they supply one.

## No vanity KPIs

Pick KPIs that measure outcomes, not activity. "Impressions" and "reach" flatter the report but don't tell you the campaign worked; "qualified signups", "pipeline influenced", and "activation rate" do.

## Review checklist (before the brief runs)

- Exactly one measurable goal, stated as a number that must move.
- A named audience segment, not "everyone".
- The fewest channels that reach that audience — no channel included out of habit.
- Every KPI has a baseline and a target, each from a named source (Rule 13).
- Budget is a real figure from the user or a visible `[BUDGET PLACEHOLDER]`.
- The brief starts with `DRAFT` until human sign-off, and nothing external ships without it.
