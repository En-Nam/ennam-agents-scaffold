# Journal-entry playbook

A journal entry records what a source document says happened — nothing more. Its integrity rests on two things: it balances, and every amount can be traced to a document. Use this skill any time you draft or review an entry.

## Debits = credits

- Every entry has at least one debit and one credit, and **total debits must equal total credits**. This is non-negotiable, not a target to approximate.
- If the entry doesn't balance, it is not finished. Surface the imbalance and find the missing or wrong line — never add a plug to force it to zero.

## Every line cites a source document

- Each line's amount is **transcribed from a source document** — an invoice, receipt, bank statement, or contract — and the entry cites which one.
- Never invent, estimate, or recall an amount from memory. The number lives in the document; you carry it across, you do not compute it (Rule 13).
- No source, no line: an amount you can't point to a document for does not get recorded.

## Account and tax code are a human judgment

- Which account an amount hits, whether it's capitalised or expensed, and which tax/VAT code applies are **judgment calls that belong to a person** (Rule 5).
- Draft the entry with a proposed classification and your reasoning, then mark it for a human to confirm. Present alternatives where the treatment is genuinely arguable.
- Don't average two plausible treatments into a hybrid — name the tension and let a human resolve it (Rule 7).

## Structure of an entry

1. **Date** — the date of the transaction (mind the period cut-off).
2. **Description** — what happened, in one line.
3. **Lines** — account, debit/credit, amount, and the source document each amount cites.
4. **Balance check** — debits total = credits total, shown explicitly.
5. **Status** — `DRAFT` until a human confirms the classification and signs off.

## Review checklist (before saving an entry)

- Total debits equal total credits, shown explicitly.
- Every line cites the source document its amount came from.
- No amount was invented, estimated, or plugged to force a balance.
- Account and tax-code choices are flagged for human confirmation, not silently decided.
- The date respects the period cut-off.
- File is marked `DRAFT` until sign-off.
